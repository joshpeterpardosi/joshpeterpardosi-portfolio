1 — The question

Which customers are about to leave, and what does that do to revenue?

A SaaS business needs both answers on an ongoing basis, not once in a notebook. So the goal was a full loop: churn labels from the Kaggle Telco dataset, a synthetic MRR series aligned to its customer cohorts, and two models that keep themselves current when incoming data stops looking like training data.

3 — The decision that mattered

0.5 is a library default, not a decision

At the default threshold the model missed roughly half of the customers who actually churned. A missed churner loses the account; a false alarm costs one retention call. Pricing a miss at ten false alarms and minimising expected cost pointed to a threshold of 0.07 — which flags 58.6% of healthy customers. A list that long gets ignored, so the saving never arrives.

The fix was to minimise cost subject to a precision floor of 0.50: at least half of flagged accounts must be real churners. That lands at 0.25. The 10:1 ratio is an assumption, not a measurement — when real costs exist, it is one constant to change. The threshold is stored with the model, so serving applies the point training chose.

ROC-AUC is threshold-free, and the registry still ranks versions by it — so choosing an operating point can't game the promotion gate.

4 — What it doesn't do yet

Limit The forecast's RMSE (38.96) barely beats a naive last-month baseline (39.06). Holding out each customer's final month puts almost every churn-to-zero event in test — about 94 in training against 1,272 in test, which carry 99.8% of the squared error.
Limit At the chosen point, half of the flagged accounts are false alarms. That is the floor by construction — acceptable for a phone call, not for an expensive retention offer.
